# YouTube AI Usage Guide

[← Back to README](../README.md)

> Two separate systems apply to AI content:
> 1. **Disclosure** (labels): tells viewers something realistic is synthetic. Free, and doesn't affect monetisation.
> 2. **Inauthentic content policy** (monetisation): decides whether you get paid. **This is the real risk.**

---

## 1. Disclosure: the "altered or synthetic content" label

### You MUST disclose when content is realistic and could be mistaken for real:
- A realistic person saying or doing something they didn't do
- Realistic footage of real places or events that's altered, or invented scenes that look real
- A synthetic voice made to sound like a **specific, recognisable real person**
- Reconstructions of real events that look real

### You do NOT need to disclose:
- AI used for **scripting, research, ideas, titles or captions**
- Clearly **unrealistic, animated or fantasy** content
- Minor edits: colour correction, filters, background blur, beauty effects
- A generic AI narrator voice that doesn't imitate any real person (a realistic-sounding voice alone does *not* trigger the rule)
- Cloning **your own** voice

### How it works now (May 2026 update)
- Set it at upload: **Studio → Details → "Altered or synthetic content" → Yes**
- Photorealistic and meaningfully AI-altered content gets a **prominent label**: directly under the player on long-form, and as an **overlay on Shorts**
- Unrealistic, animated or slightly altered content gets the label in the **expanded description** only
- **YouTube now auto-detects and auto-labels** undisclosed AI content. You can't remove a label YouTube applies, and repeatedly failing to disclose can lead to removal or YPP suspension.
- Content made with YouTube's own gen-AI tools (e.g. Veo in Shorts) is labelled automatically

**Rule:** when in doubt, disclose. The label costs nothing; getting caught not disclosing does.

## 2. Monetisation: the inauthentic content policy

In **July 2025** YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to **"inauthentic content"**. In **mid-2026** it tightened the policy and named three categories that **can't be monetised**:

1. **Generic / mass-produced AI videos:** templated, repetitive or near-identical uploads at scale
2. **Distressing or manipulative content:** emotionally exploitative clips made for clicks
3. **AI personas giving advice on sensitive topics:** for example health or finance, delivered by synthetic presenters

YouTube's stated position is that **AI tools are welcome** and AI-assisted channels stay eligible **if they add real value**. Reviewers judge the **overall character of the channel**, not one video in isolation.

### Safe vs. risky AI use

| ✅ Monetisation-safe | ❌ Monetisation risk |
|---|---|
| AI drafts → **human-edited** script with a unique angle | Fully AI script read out unchanged |
| AI clips as **B-roll** within an edited story | Slideshows of AI images + TTS, same template every time |
| Original research, data or analysis | Rewording Wikipedia or other videos |
| Consistent editorial voice and format | 5–20 near-identical uploads per day |
| Human fact-checking | AI "doctor" or "financial advisor" avatars |
| AI voice reading **your** original script | Reaction or compilation videos with no real commentary |

### Our pipeline rule
> **AI does the labour; a human owns the judgement.**
> Every video needs at least: a human-chosen topic & angle, a human-edited script, human fact-checking, and a human final review before publishing.

## Third-party AI tool checks
- **Commercial-use rights:** many free tiers ban commercial use, and a monetised channel counts as commercial
- **Watermarks:** never publish watermarked clips
- **Likeness and voice:** never generate real people (celebrities, politicians) without consent. It's a privacy and impersonation risk, not just a labelling one.
- **AI music:** rights are still unsettled. Prefer licensed tracks or the YouTube Audio Library.
