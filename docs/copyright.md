# YouTube Copyright Guide

[← Back to README](../README.md)

> Faceless channels stack lots of third-party assets (music, footage, images), so each video has more ways to trigger a copyright problem. Use this as a guide, not legal advice.

---

## Claim vs. strike: know the difference

| | Content ID claim | Copyright strike |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Automated match against a rights holder's registered asset (or a manual claim) | A legal copyright removal (takedown) request that YouTube judges valid |
| **Video** | Stays up (usually). The owner can monetise, track or block it | **Removed** |
| **Channel penalty** | None directly | Yes. Counts against the channel |
| **Fix** | Trim or replace the claimed part, swap the audio, or dispute | Wait 90 days + Copyright School, ask for a retraction, or counter-notify |

**Shorts note:** a music claim on a Short can effectively make it disappear, while long-form videos usually stay live with revenue redirected to the claimant.

## The strike rules
- **1 strike:** video removed; you must complete **Copyright School**
- A strike **expires after 90 days** if Copyright School is done and there are fewer than 3 active strikes
- **3 active strikes within 90 days → channel termination.** All videos deleted, linked channels at risk, and you can't create new channels.
- **Scheduled removal:** some notices give you **7 days** to remove the content yourself and avoid the strike. Act fast.

## Disputes: be careful
- Dispute a Content ID claim **only if you're confident** you own the content or have a licence, or it's clearly fair use.
- The claimant has ~30 days to respond to a dispute. If you appeal a rejected dispute, they can file a **takedown → strike**. Reckless disputes turn claims into strikes.
- A counter-notification is a **legal process**: your personal details go to the claimant, and it can lead to a lawsuit.

## Safe asset sources
| Asset | Safer options |
|---|---|
| Music | YouTube Audio Library, properly licensed stock music services. *"Content ID-cleared" ≠ copyright-free: you still need a licence* |
| Footage | Your own AI-generated clips (check the tool's commercial-use rights), Pexels/Pixabay, paid stock libraries |
| Images | Licensed stock, your own AI images, public-domain archives (check each item's status) |
| Fonts | Google Fonts or properly licensed fonts (thumbnails count) |

## Fair use: the short version
Fair use is a **legal defence decided case by case**, not a rule YouTube applies automatically. The factors are: purpose (transformative commentary, criticism or education), nature of the work, how much you used, and effect on the original's market. "I gave credit," "it's only 10 seconds," or "I'm not monetising" do **not** make something fair use.

## High-risk habits (faceless channels)
- ❌ Movie/TV clips, sports footage, music videos
- ❌ Re-uploading other creators' videos, even "with commentary" that's just a voiceover reading facts
- ❌ Trending pop songs as background music
- ❌ Downloading "no copyright" compilations from random YouTube channels
- ❌ News footage from broadcasters

## Protect your own content
- Keep a **licence log** (asset → source → licence → date) for every video. It's your evidence if you're claimed.
- Once you qualify, consider YouTube's **Copyright Match Tool** to find re-uploads of your videos.
